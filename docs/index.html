DATA, DEFINITIONS & REUSE

Behind the measure.

What does mismatch measure?

For each county and year, low-income ZCTAs are identified by ranking ZCTAs by median household income and selecting ranks with rank / N ≤ 0.25.

Mismatch = 1 − large establishments in low-income ZCTAs
all large establishments in the county

Large establishments have at least 250 employees. The ratio counts establishments, not employees. A value of 1 means the recorded large establishments are all outside the selected low-income ZCTAs. It does not mean the county has no employers.

Annual values and original analysis references

The annual table uses the prepared CBP source for the same 404 study counties. All six years from 2011 through 2016 are available: 2,424 observations. The original CJARS-based baseline panel retained 2,219 of those annual observations.

The original analysis mean averages a county’s available 2011–2016 values within that original panel. The complete six-year mean averages all six years from the indicator source. These definitions differ for 51 counties. The original high/low group is preserved, using a cutoff of 0.7431652546; it is not recalculated from the current year or the comparison selection.

Using complete means with the original cutoff changes five groups. The explorer preserves the original groups.

Missing years and the 2017 reporting change

2010 has no CBP mismatch in this project. Other unavailable values have a zero denominator in the prepared source, meaning no usable reported count of large establishments. This must not be interpreted as confirmed absence of large employers.

Beginning with reference year 2017, Census publishes a cell only if it contains at least three establishments. Earlier and later values are not fully comparable. The chart separates lines at this break. Census CBP methodology ↗
